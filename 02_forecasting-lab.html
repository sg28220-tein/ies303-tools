1 · Level methods

demand fluctuates around a stable average — the forecast smooths out the noise

Moving Average

Ft+1 = (At + At−1 + … + At−k+1) / k
k3
Larger k = smoother but slower to react · k = 1 is the naive forecast. First forecast at period k+1.

Weighted Moving Average

Ft+1 = w1At + w2At−1 + … + wkAt−k+1
w₁ = newest period
Weights are normalized to sum to 1. Heavier weight on recent periods reacts faster; equal weights = simple MA.

Simple Exponential Smoothing

Ft+1 = Ft + α (At − Ft)  ·  start naive: F2 = A1
α0.30
Low α = smooth & slow to react · high α = responsive & nervous · α = 1 is the naive forecast.

2 · Trend

systematic growth or decline — project the slope

Trend-Adjusted (TAF)

TAFt+1 = St + Tt
St = TAFt + α (At − TAFt)
Tt = Tt−1 + β (TAFt − TAFt−1 − Tt−1)
initialize on periods 1–2: TAF2 = A2, S2 = A2, T2 = A2 − A1
α0.30
β0.20
Initial trend T2 = A2 − A1 = – — read off the data, not tuned
St smooths the level (same as simple ES); β smooths the trend. Periods 1 and 2 are used up by initialization, so the first forecast error is at period 3.

3 · Seasonality

repeating pattern every m periods

Holt–Winters (seasonal)

Lt = α (At / St−m) + (1−α)(Lt−1 + Tt−1)
Tt = β (Lt − Lt−1) + (1−β) Tt−1
St = γ (At / Lt) + (1−γ) St−m
Ft+1 = (Lt + Tt) · St+1−m
α0.30
β0.10
γ0.20
Extends the lecture's seasonal relatives: the seasonal indices St are re-estimated by smoothing (γ) instead of staying fixed. Level L, trend T as before. First forecast after one full season.

Actual vs forecast

Residual plot

Residual et = At − Ft. Points scattered randomly around zero = the method has captured the pattern. Runs of same-sign residuals, a drift, or a repeating wave mean something is left unexplained (trend or seasonality).

Forecast accuracy

MAD = Σ|e| / n · MSE = Σe² / (n−1) · MAPE = Σ(|e|/A)·100 / n — over periods that have a forecast.
